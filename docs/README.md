## OctoAcme Project Management Approach

OctoAcme's project management approach is built around a clear lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and closeout. The project docs emphasize that every initiative starts with a validated problem statement, stakeholder alignment, and a lightweight one-pager that defines the business need, desired outcomes, success metrics, timeline, and resource assumptions. Once the project is approved, teams move into planning, where backlog items are prioritized, acceptance criteria are documented, dependencies and risks are identified, and a release plan with milestones is established. This keeps work grounded in measurable outcomes and ensures that teams are aligned before execution begins.

The operating model defines a set of core roles and responsibilities that support cross-functional delivery. Product managers own the problem, priorities, and success metrics; project managers coordinate schedules, risks, resources, and communication; developers build and test features in line with acceptance criteria; QA validates quality and acceptance; and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and sponsorship. The docs also describe a shared set of artifacts such as the project charter, release plan, risk register, acceptance criteria, backlog, and retrospectives. These artifacts create a single source of truth and make it easier for teams to coordinate across functions while staying focused on outcomes and customer value.

Communication and governance are structured around regular rhythms and clear escalation paths. The team uses daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, milestone reviews, and periodic stakeholder updates to keep momentum and surface blockers early. Risks and dependencies are tracked in a risk register, reviewed in weekly syncs, and escalated through defined layers—from the team and PM to the product lead and sponsor when issues become business-impacting. The communication templates in the docs focus on progress, next steps, risks, decisions needed, and incident summaries, helping teams keep stakeholders informed without losing accountability or creating confusion.

Quality assurance is treated as an ongoing part of delivery, not a final gate. The docs recommend unit, integration, and end-to-end testing depending on scope, along with CI checks for tests, linting, and security scanning. Acceptance criteria and Definition of Done are used to guide work and ensure that features are only considered complete when they meet the agreed standard. Releases follow a consistent checklist that includes staging validation, smoke testing, deployment verification, and communication to stakeholders. After each sprint or major milestone, the team conducts retrospectives to capture what went well, what needs improvement, and which actions should be tracked in the backlog to support continuous improvement.

## Core Principles

- **Customer-first**: Prioritize customer value and usability
- **Iterative delivery**: Deliver small, testable increments
- **Clear ownership**: Each project has a named PM and Product Lead
- **Data-informed decisions**: Measure impact and iterate based on evidence
- **Psychological safety**: Encourage feedback and learning
